Storyboard for the redesigned DermAI app

Every screen in the next version of the app, for both people who use it: patients who scan and care for their skin, and dermatologists who review cases, chat with patients and run their schedule. One app, two roles chosen at sign-up. Screens are numbered in the order a person meets them.

Brand and system

Clinical calm with one signature: the six Fitzpatrick skin tones, the scale dermatologists use to classify skin. They form the logo and the skin-tone picker and say, quietly, that the app is built for every skin. Full rules are in design.md.

A

Getting started Shared

Everyone meets the same five screens. The role chosen on A3 decides whether the next step is a skin profile (B) or credential checks (G).

B

Skin profile Patient

Three short steps that shape every result and routine. Skin tone comes first because the model and the advice both depend on it.

C

Scan and results Patient

The heart of the app. Quality checks run live in the camera so bad photos never reach the model, and results lead with a face map that shows where each concern sits.

D

Everyday use Patient

The four tabs a patient lives in. Scan sits in the centre because a new scan is always one tap away.

E

Care: AI and dermatologists Patient

The Care tab joins the AI assistant and real doctors in one place. The AI answers everyday questions and hands off to a dermatologist when something needs one.

F

Me and settings Patient

Profile, history, notifications and privacy controls, including deleting data.

G

Dermatologist sign-up Dermatologist

After A3 to A5, a dermatologist proves their registration before seeing any patient. Dermatologist screens carry a violet accent so it's always clear which side of the app you're on.

H

Dermatologist workspace Dermatologist

Five tabs: Today, Cases, Schedule, Messages, Me. Case review puts the patient's photo, the AI's reading and the doctor's own call side by side; the doctor can always override the AI.
