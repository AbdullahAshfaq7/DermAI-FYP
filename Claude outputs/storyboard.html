Brand and system

The mark is a camera aperture made of the six Fitzpatrick skin tones, the scale dermatologists use to classify skin, around a violet lens. It says the app looks at every skin, with no slogan needed.

A

Getting started Shared

Five screens everyone sees. The role picked on A3 decides what comes next: a skin profile (B) for patients, credential checks (G) for dermatologists.

B

Skin profile Patient

Three short steps that shape every result and routine. Skin tone comes first because the model and the advice both depend on it.

C

Scan and results Patient

The core of the app, and the one place the design gets loud. The flow goes dark so the skin is the brightest thing on screen. Quality checks run live in the camera, and results open with a glowing map of where each concern sits.

D

Everyday use Patient

The screens a patient comes back to daily. The floating dock keeps Scan in the middle and within thumb reach. Routine opens on the right half of the day by itself.

E

Care: AI and dermatologists Patient

The Care tab puts the AI assistant and real doctors in one place. The AI answers everyday questions. When something needs a doctor, it says so and offers an open slot right there.

F

Me and settings Patient

Profile, history, saved items and privacy. Deleting data is plain to find, and so is stopping a doctor from seeing your scans.

G

Dermatologist sign-up Dermatologist

After A3 to A5, a dermatologist proves their registration before seeing any patient. Every dermatologist screen uses emerald in place of violet, so it's always clear which side of the app you're on.

H

Dermatologist workspace Dermatologist

Five tabs: Today, Cases, Schedule, Messages and Me. Case review puts the photo, the AI's reading and the patient's own words side by side, and the doctor always has the final say.
